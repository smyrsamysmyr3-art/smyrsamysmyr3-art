## UST2026

UST2026 is a continuing research program developing and testing a proposed framework for Universal Stasis Theory, Hosting Time, and Environmental Ascent Clocks.

The project is structured as a continuous research program rather than a collection of independent papers.

The current integrated development consists of **Parts I–VI**.

---

## Research Program

### Universal Stasis Theory (UST)

UST proposes an alternative mechanical framework for describing motion and equilibrium through environmental interactions and medium-dependent effects.

The framework is developed independently from the assumption that gravity must be the fundamental mechanism of the proposed dynamics.

### Two-Scale Hosting Time

The Hosting Time framework investigates an operational relationship between local timekeeping and a proposed environmental or hosting-time scale.

The research distinguishes between physical time, operational clocks, reference processes, and environmental calibration.

### Environmental Ascent Clock Theory

The Environmental Ascent Clock framework defines a clock operationally through:

- a carrier
- an environment
- a reference
- an observable cyclic process
- a continuous counter
- an explicit calibration procedure

The framework includes the Environmental Calendar and the Galactic Environmental Clock construction.

---

## Parts I–VI

The research program has been developed progressively through six connected parts.

**Part I** — Initial theoretical framework and foundations.

**Part II** — Physical and operational development of the Hosting Time hypothesis.

**Part III** — Continued theoretical development and computational formulation.

**Part IV** — Integration of the accumulated framework and preparation for reproducible testing.

**Part V** — Environmental Ascent Clock Theory, astronomical realization, phase unwrapping, uncertainty propagation, and reproducible testing.

**Part VI** — Complete integrated closure of the Hosting Clock framework, local–host transfer, and a direct Hubble-scale testing program.

Part VI is a continuation and closure of the preceding work and is not intended as an independent restart.

## Environmental Ascent Clock

The Galactic Environmental Clock defines an operational astronomical clock using the Solar System barycenter as the carrier, the Milky Way as the environment, the Galactic center as the reference, and galactocentric azimuth as the observable cyclic process.

The framework explicitly addresses phase wrapping and establishes sampling conditions required for safe phase unwrapping.

The methodology includes:

- deterministic sampling bounds
- uncertainty-aware sampling
- branch-clearance conditions
- adaptive acceptance criteria
- phase unwrapping
- uncertainty propagation
- reproducible data processing

## Hubble-Scale Testing

Using the retained reference value:

**H_early = 67.4 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹**

the fixed clock transformation gives:

**H_clock = 68.5967604235 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹**

with the rounded reporting value:

**H_clock ≈ 68.5968 km s⁻¹ Mpc⁻¹**

Within the research framework, this value is treated as a prediction generated from the retained clock factor, not as a fitted value.

The decisive future test is the redshift-dependent expansion history **H(z)** rather than a comparison based only on a single H₀ value.

## BAO and Cosmological Testing

The Part VI framework defines a reproducible pathway for testing the proposed clock transformation against BAO observables.

The testing architecture includes:

- H(z) construction
- line-of-sight distance
- transverse comoving distance
- sound-horizon normalization
- observational data vectors
- full covariance matrices
- χ² evaluation
- model-comparison statistics

The methodology requires the complete observational covariance matrix, including off-diagonal terms, whenever such a covariance matrix is available.

No missing observational covariance matrix is fabricated.

---

## Numerical Validation

The project also contains numerical experiments designed to test mathematical and computational properties of the proposed dynamical framework.

These numerical results are explicitly distinguished from observational confirmation.

A successful numerical experiment demonstrates behavior of the implemented mathematical model under specified conditions. It does not, by itself, establish that the model describes nature.

---

## Scientific Integrity

The research follows the methodological sequence:

**derive → freeze → predict → test → accept or reject**

Important distinctions are maintained between:

- theoretical derivation
- numerical validation
- observational testing
- calibration
- prediction
- empirical confirmation

Missing observational results are not replaced by assumed or fabricated values.

Historical diagonal tests, where retained, are identified as historical controls rather than substitutes for complete observational covariance testing.

---

## Reproducibility

The project is intended to provide a reproducible research pathway.

Where applicable, the research records:

- equations and definitions
- frozen parameters
- numerical procedures
- sampling conditions
- uncertainty treatment
- data requirements
- statistical procedures
- acceptance or rejection criteria

The computational framework is intended to allow independent researchers to reproduce the numerical experiments and perform the required observational tests when the necessary datasets are available.

---

## What UST2026 Claims

UST2026 presents a developing theoretical and computational research framework.

The project documents:

- theoretical constructions
- operational definitions
- mathematical formulations
- numerical experiments
- proposed observational tests
- falsifiable predictions

The project does not treat an unperformed observational test as completed.

---

## What UST2026 Does Not Claim

UST2026 does not claim that numerical experiments alone establish physical reality.

It does not fabricate missing observational covariance results.

It does not present proposed observational tests as completed measurements.

It does not treat agreement with a single cosmological number as sufficient validation of the complete framework.

The ultimate evaluation of the framework depends on reproducible mathematical, computational, and observational testing.
